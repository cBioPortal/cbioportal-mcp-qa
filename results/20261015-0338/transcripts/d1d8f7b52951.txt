For treatment-outcome questions (e.g. immunotherapy response), say this first; the only proxies are `OS_MONTHS`/`OS_STATUS`, or NLP radiology progression events (`Diagnosis` events with `SUBTYPE = 'Progression'`, key `PROGRESSION` = Y/N/Indeterminate), in patients with `Treatment` events of the relevant `SUBTYPE` (e.g. `Immuno`: 3,341 patients). There is no meaningful same-patient (paired) primary-vs-metastasis cohort. SELECT countIf(n > 1) AS multi_sample_patients,         -- 90
       countIf(has_p AND has_m) AS primary_and_met      -- 26

             has(groupArray(attribute_value), 'Primary') AS has_p,

      WHERE cancer_study_identifier = 'msk_chord_2024' AND attribute_name = 'SAMPLE_TYPE'
      GROUP BY patient_unique_id);

## Answer

**26 unique patients** in the MSK‑CHORD Study (MSK, Nature 2024) have both a `Primary` and a `Metastasis` sample.

For context:
- **15,872** patients have at least one `Primary` sample
- **8,872** patients have at least one `Metastasis` sample
- Only **26** patients have both

This is a very small paired cohort (the study is nearly one sample per patient: 24,950 patients / 25,040 samples). So if you're thinking about a **paired** primary‑vs‑metastasis comparison, note that only these 26 patients qualify. Most primary-vs-metastasis analyses in this study must be done as an **unpaired** comparison of all `Primary` vs `Metastasis` samples — I can set that up, or link you to the study cohort, if you'd like.
