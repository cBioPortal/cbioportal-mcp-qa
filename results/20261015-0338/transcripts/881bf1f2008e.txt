## On whether TP53 mutations are "clinically actionable"

**First, the framing caveat:** "Clinically actionable" usually means a variant that can be acted on at the patient level — i.e., a *targetable* alteration (one a drug or regimen is directed at) or one that changes treatment selection / predicts response.

**Second, a key data point you should know:** cBioPortal stores the presence/absence and basic *** of TP53 mutations (the `genomic_event` table has a `driver` flag), but it does **not** store OncoKB or OncoKB-derived clinical-actionability annotations ("OncoKBMut", "Oncogenic", "LikelyOncogenic", "VUS"). So I cannot quote a cBioPortal-derived actionability label, and I should not conflate "TP53 is frequently mutated / annotated as a driver in some studies" with "this is clinically targeted."

**General-knowledge framing (not from cBioPortal, standard in the oncology literature):**
- TP53 is most often described as a *tumor-suppressor/driver* gene — it is **highly frequent** in many cancers, but it is generally **not a directly druggable target** in the way, e.g., an EGFR exon-19 deletion is.
- Its clinical significance is more context-dependent — it can be a **biomarker** (e.g., association with immunotherapy response or prognosis) rather than a target in its own right.
- Whether a *specific* TP53 variant is "actionable" is a patient-level question that hinges on the individual variant, cancer type, and current NCCN/ESMO-style guidelines — that is precisely where I **cannot** advise.
