What I can tell you, factually and outside of cBioPortal data (general knowledge, not sourced from the database):

- TP53 is a tumor suppressor gene, and most TP53 mutations are loss-of-function, unlike gain-of-function oncogene mutations (e.g., EGFR, BRAF) for which targeted inhibitors exist.
- There is currently no FDA-approved targeted therapy specifically for TP53-mutant tumors in routine clinical use, in contrast to genes like EGFR, ALK, BRAF, or IDH1 that have approved matched therapies.
- TP53 mutation status is used prognostically and in some clinical trial eligibility criteria, and there are investigational agents (e.g., mutant p53 reactivators) in trials, but this is not the same as established clinical actionability.
