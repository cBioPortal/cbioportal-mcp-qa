IDH1 (isocitrate dehydrogenase 1) mutations are recurrent, functionally well-characterized alterations found in several cancer types, most notably gliomas (especially lower-grade gliomas), acute myeloid leukemia (AML), chondrosarcoma, and cholangiocarcinoma.

**What the mutations do biologically** (general knowledge, not retrieved from cBioPortal data):

- IDH1 normally encodes an enzyme that converts isocitrate to α-ketoglutarate (α-KG) in the cytoplasm.
- The recurrent mutations (nearly always at codon **R132**, e.g., R132H) are gain-of-function: the mutant enzyme acquires a new (neomorphic) activity that converts α-KG into **2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG)**, an "oncometabolite."
- 2-HG accumulates to high levels and competitively inhibits α-KG-dependent dioxygenases, including TET enzymes and histone demethylases, leading to widespread **DNA and histone hypermethylation** and blocked cellular differentiation.
- In glioma, IDH1 mutation is an early driver event, strongly associated with the CpG island methylator phenotype (G-CIMP), and is a favorable prognostic marker relative to IDH-wildtype disease.
- In AML, IDH1 mutations contribute to a differentiation block in myeloid progenitors and are targetable — mutant-IDH1 inhibitors (e.g., ivosidenib) are used clinically.
