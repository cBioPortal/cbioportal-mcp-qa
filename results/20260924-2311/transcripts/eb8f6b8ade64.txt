What I *can* offer, from general knowledge (not sourced from cBioPortal, so please verify against primary literature/clinical guidelines):

- **Prognostic significance:** MGMT (O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase) repairs alkylating-agent-induced DNA damage. When its promoter is hypermethylated, the gene is silenced, reducing DNA repair capacity in tumor cells.
- **Treatment response:** This is thought to make tumor cells more sensitive to alkylating chemotherapy (notably temozolomide). Patients with MGMT promoter methylation have historically shown improved response and longer survival with temozolomide plus radiotherapy compared to unmethylated tumors.
- **Prognosis:** MGMT methylation status is generally considered a favorable prognostic marker in glioblastoma, independent of treatment.
