**IDH1** (isocitrate dehydrogenase 1) is a cytoplasmic enzyme that normally converts isocitrate to α-ketoglutarate (α-KG) while producing NADPH. Cancer-associated IDH1 mutations are typically heterozygous, change a single arginine in the active site (most often R132, with R132H the most common), and give the enzyme a new activity.

**What the mutations do:**

1. **Neomorphic enzyme activity:** The mutant enzyme reduces α-KG to **D-2-hydroxyglutarate (D-2HG)**, which accumulates to very high levels in tumor cells.
2. **Oncometabolite effect:** D-2HG structurally resembles α-KG and inhibits α-KG-dependent dioxygenases, including TET DNA demethylases and Jumonji-family histone demethylases.
3. **Epigenetic reprogramming:** Inhibiting these enzymes produces widespread DNA and histone hypermethylation (a CpG island methylator phenotype). This is thought to block cell differentiation, which contributes to tumorigenesis.

**Where they are common:** IDH1 mutations are recurrent in diffuse gliomas, acute myeloid leukemia, and cholangiocarcinoma. In gliomas, IDH-mutant tumors form a distinct molecular class and are generally associated with better prognosis than IDH-wildtype tumors.
