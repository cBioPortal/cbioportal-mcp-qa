# Mutation Frequency Analysis Guide

## IMPORTANT: Reporting Mutation Frequencies
- **ALWAYS report frequencies as percentages**, not raw counts: `frequency = (altered_samples / total_profiled_samples) × 100`
- For quick frequency lookups, **prefer the TCGA Pan-Cancer Atlas study first**, then offer to expand to other studies
- When reporting across multiple studies, show **ranges** (e.g., "TP53 is mutated in 30–60% of samples") rather than a single average
- **NEVER** sum mutation events across studies to compute an aggregate frequency — this can exceed 100% due to double-counting
- Warn users that samples may overlap across cohorts (e.g., MSK studies may share patients)
- **Choose and state the counting unit**: use patient-level frequencies for prevalence/rate questions unless the user explicitly asks for samples; use sample-level frequencies when the user asks about samples.
- **For "across cancer types" questions**, jump to the [Cross-Cancer-Type Mutation Frequency](#cross-cancer-type-mutation-frequency) section below — there is one correct recipe and several common wrong ones.

## Counting Unit: Samples vs Patients

Before answering any mutation count or frequency question, decide whether the unit is samples or patients and state that choice in the answer.

| User wording | Counting unit |
|--------------|---------------|
| "prevalence", "rate", "fraction of patients", "patients with", "how common is" | Patient-level: `COUNT(DISTINCT patient_unique_id)` |
| "samples", "specimens", "biopsies", sample-level cohort composition | Sample-level: `COUNT(DISTINCT sample_unique_id)` |
| Ambiguous | Ask, or default to patient-level for prevalence/rate language and say so |

### Cross-study sample-count caveat

When an answer touches more than one study and reports a sample count, prepend a one-line caveat:

> Sample IDs are unique within cBioPortal study prefixes, not guaranteed biological-sample identifiers across studies; overlapping cohorts can count the same patient/sample more than once.

Prefer one of these safer approaches:

- Use a shipped `cancer_study_query_preferences` cohort such as `pan_cancer_tcga` or `all_studies_non_redundant`.
- Restrict to one named study.
- Aggregate by `patient_unique_id` when the biological question is patient prevalence.

## STOP rule: a frequency above 100% means your query is wrong

If your query returns a frequency over 100%, **do not try to debug or explain the data inconsistency to the user**. The cause is always one of these query bugs:

- Summing mutation events instead of `COUNT(DISTINCT sample_unique_id)` for the numerator
- Using a study-wide sample count as the denominator instead of the gene-specific profiled count
- Cross-study aggregation where the same biological sample appears under multiple `sample_unique_id` values (e.g., MSK-IMPACT and MSK-CHORD share patients)
- **Joining the profiled CTE through `gene_panel` / `gene_panel_list` without a WES branch.** `gene_panel_id = 'WES'` is *not* a row in … (35166 more chars)

## Key Genomic Differences Between Lung Adenocarcinoma and Squamous Cell Carcinoma (TCGA PanCancer Atlas)

Based on analysis of 566 LUAD and 487 LUSC samples, here are the major genomic distinctions:

---

### **1. TP53 Mutation Burden (Strongest Differentiator)**
- **LUSC: 83.5%** (404/484 samples) — nearly universal
- **LUAD: 52.1%** (295/566 samples) — moderate enrichment
- **Difference: +31.4 percentage points in LUSC**

TP53 loss is a hallmark of squamous differentiation, making it the most striking genomic signature.

---

### **2. Actionable Oncogene Mutations (LUAD-Enriched)**

| Gene | LUAD | LUSC | Difference |
|------|------|------|-----------|
| **EGFR** | 12.4% | 2.9% | +9.5 pp (4× higher in LUAD) |
| **STK11/LKB1** | 13.3% | 1.0% | +12.3 pp (13× higher in LUAD) |
| **KEAP1** | 18.0% | 10.1% | +7.9 pp (1.8× higher in LUAD) |
| **KRAS** | 29.7% | 31.1% | Similar (~30% in both) |

**Clinical insight:** EGFR mutations define a therapeutically actionable subset predominantly in LUAD; STK11 loss (LKB1) is a strong LUAD marker associated with immunotherapy resistance.

---

### **3. Copy Number Alterations: Divergent Focal Amplicons**

**LUAD (16.8% CDKN2A/CDKN2B HOMDEL):**
- Focal homozygous deletions of the **9p21 locus** (CDKN2A/CDKN2B): 16.8%
- Focal **14q13 amplifications** (NKX2-1 axis, SFTA3, MBIP): 13.1–13.3%
- NKX2-1 (TTF-1) amplification: 13.1% — lineage-specific transcription factor

**LUSC (40% 3q26–3q27 mega-amplicon):**
- **Massive 3q amplification** affecting SOX2, ATP11B, DCUN1D1, LAMP3 block: **~40%**
- This 3q mega-amplicon is a hallmark LUSC lesion
- Less frequent CDKN2A deletions: 0% (not in top-10 CNA list for LUSC)
- **vs. LUAD:** only 16.8%

**Clinical insight:** The 3q amplicon in LUSC drives squamous differentiation markers (SOX2 oncogene); the 14q NKX2-1 amplification in LUAD reflects adenocarcinoma lineage dependency.

---

### **4. Structural Variants & Fusions**

**LUAD fusion spectrum (driven by smoking-exposed epithelium):**
- **SFTPB fusions**: 3.3% (surfactant-associated, adenocarcinoma-specific)
- **ALK fusions**: 1.0% (5/511 samples; actionable target)
- **ROS1**: 1.4%
- Other: EML4, ASH1L, CHD6

**LUSC fusion spectrum (sparse):**
- **FGFR3, TACC3, MARK3 fusions**: 1.2% each
- **No ALK or ROS1 fusions** reported
- Overall SV burden much lower

**Clinical insight:** Tyrosine kinase fusions (ALK, ROS1) are LUAD-specific druggable events; LUSC lacks the fusion-prone transcriptomic landscape.

---

### **5. Mutation Frequency Spectrum**

**Top 3 mutated genes (sample level):**

| Rank | LUAD | LUSC |
|------|------|------|
| 1 | TP53 (52.1%) | TP53 (83.5%) |
| 2 | TTN (48.1%) — passenger | TTN (71.3%) |
| 3 | MUC16 (42.8%) — passenger | CSMD3 (43.2%) |

TTN and MUC16 are large genes frequently mutated by chance in high-mutation-rate cancers; CSMD3 ranks higher in LUSC, suggesting different mutational pressure or selection.

---

### **6. Mutational vs. Structural Genomic Phenotypes**

- **LUAD:** More balanced—point mutations + fusions + selective focal CNAs (NKX2-1 AMP, CDKN2A DEL)
- **LUSC:** Mutation-dominated with massive focal amplicons (3q), universal TP53 loss, and rare fusions

### Summary
The adenocarcinoma vs. squamous distinction is encoded in **oncogene context** (EGFR/STK11 in LUAD; 3q SOX2 in LUSC) and **lineage-specific CNAs** (14q NKX2-1 in LUAD; 3q mega-amp in LUSC), not primarily in TP53 frequency alone. STK11 loss is a powerful LUAD marker predictive of immunotherapy resistance, while LUSC's nearly-universal TP53 mutation and SOX2 amplification define its molecular identity.
