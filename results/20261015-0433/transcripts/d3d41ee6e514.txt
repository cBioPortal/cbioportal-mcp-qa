I can't check the paper's methods against the portal data, and this answer is from my own recollection of Jee et al. (Nature 2024), not from cBioPortal. Please verify any specific details against the Methods and Supplementary Information. These are the weaknesses I'd raise, roughly in order of how much they matter.

**1. Survival bias from late sequencing (left truncation / immortal time)**
- Patients enter the cohort only after MSK-IMPACT sequencing, which often happens months or years after diagnosis. Anyone who died before being sequenced is never seen.
- If survival is counted from diagnosis without delayed-entry (left-truncation) handling, the models are biased toward longer survival and the hazard ratios are distorted. This matters most for genomic associations, because sequencing timing is itself linked to disease course.
- It is worth checking how the paper handled the time origin and whether the main analyses used delayed entry.

**2. Selection and generalizability**
- The cohort is one tertiary cancer center. Patients sequenced there are more likely to have advanced or refractory disease, to be referred, to have better access to care, and to be eligible for trials.
- Performance may not transfer to community oncology settings, other health systems or other countries.
- Cancer type coverage is uneven. Deep modeling focused on a handful of common cancers, and the less common types have little power.

**3. NLP-derived labels**
- Many key features (metastatic sites, receptor status, PD-L1, smoking history, progression) are extracted from free-text notes and radiology reports by models. Errors in these labels flow into both the predictors and some outcomes.
- Validation against manual curation is usually on a limited, institution-specific sample. Note-writing styles and templates change over time and between clinicians, so extraction accuracy may drift. Errors may also not be random. They may be more common in complex cases, which can bias downstream associations.
- A report that doesn't mention a metastasis is not evidence that none exists. Imaging frequency varies, so "absence" may reflect "not imaged".

**4. Treatment confounding**
- Treatment and outcome data are observational. Associations between a treatment (for example, immunotherapy) and outcome are confounded by indication, line of therapy, performance status and the time-varying decision to treat.
- Time-varying exposures need careful handling (for example, landmark or time-dependent covariates). Otherwise the results are susceptible to immortal time bias. Unmeasured confounders such as comorbidities, socioeconomic factors and adherence are mostly invisible here.

**5. Prediction-model evaluation**
- Check whether performance was evaluated on a truly held-out set, ideally temporally or externally split. Random splits within one institution overstate real-world performance.
- Concordance-style metrics can hide poor calibration, and a model that beats a clinical-only baseline by a small margin may not be clinically useful.
- Ask whether the incremental gain from NLP features reflects genuine new information or proxies for disease burden or stage that were already partly captured.

**6. Genotype–phenotype discovery claims**
- Associations such as a specific gene mutation with metastatic pattern or outcome come from many tests across genes and sites. It is important to confirm that they were multiple-testing corrected and replicated in an independent cohort.
- Panel sequencing (MSK-IMPACT) covers only a fixed gene set. Different panel versions over time create differences in what was assessed. Tumor purity, sample type (primary vs. metastasis) and sample timing relative to treatment also vary. Treatment-induced and acquired alterations in post-treatment samples can be mistaken for prognostic ones.
- Patients with multiple samples need careful patient-level handling.

**7. Outcome definitions**
- Overall survival is robust, but progression or response endpoints derived from text are noisier.
- Loss to follow-up can be informative. Patients who transfer care elsewhere and are lost to follow-up are not random, so censoring may be informative.

**8. Data and reproducibility**
- The full clinical-text-derived data cannot be shared because of privacy, so independent replication of the NLP pipeline is limited. Only the derived data are available.
- Models trained on MSK text may not work on other institutions' notes without retraining.

**What I'd check first in the paper**
1. Whether the survival analyses used left truncation, and what the time origin was.
2. Held-out validation design (temporal or external vs. random split).
3. The NLP accuracy numbers by label, and how label noise was handled downstream.
4. Whether treatment effects were estimated with time-varying methods.
