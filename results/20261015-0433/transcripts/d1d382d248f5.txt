This is general background, not something I pulled from cBioPortal data or the literature.

- **Hotspot mutations:** Cancer-associated IDH1 mutations mostly hit one residue, R132 (R132H is the most common). They are heterozygous missense changes.
- **Loss of normal function:** The mutant enzyme can no longer efficiently convert isocitrate to α-ketoglutarate (α-KG).
- **New function:** It gains the ability to convert α-KG to the "oncometabolite" D-2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG), which builds up to very high levels.
- **Epigenetic effects:** 2-HG competitively inhibits α-KG-dependent enzymes, including TET DNA demethylases and histone demethylases. The result is DNA and histone hypermethylation (a CpG island methylator phenotype, or G-CIMP) and a block in cell differentiation.
- **Other reported effects:** These include altered redox balance and NADPH production, and changes in cellular metabolism.
- **Where it occurs:** IDH1 mutations are best known in lower-grade gliomas and secondary glioblastoma. They also occur in a subset of AML, chondrosarcoma and cholangiocarcinoma.
